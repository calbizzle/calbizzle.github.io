How it's calculated

  1. Tax with business = income tax on (profit + other income) − LITO + Medicare levy.
  2. Tax without business = the same calculation on other income alone.
  3. Small business offset = 16% × basic tax × (profit ÷ total taxable income), capped at $1,000. This is a simplified proportion.
  4. Set-aside = tax with business − tax without business − offset.
  5. Total = set-aside + GST collected.

Worked example

$90,000 profit and no other income: tax of $17,520 plus $1,800 Medicare comes to $19,320. Less the $1,000 small business offset, that's $18,320, or 20.4% of profit. Put aside about $1,527 a month.

FAQ

How much tax should a sole trader put aside?

There's no fixed percentage, because tax is progressive and depends on your other income. Use your profit (income minus deductible expenses), not your turnover. This calculator estimates the extra tax your business profit adds.

Do I set aside the GST I collect?

Yes, but keep it separate. GST you collect is paid to the ATO on your BAS and isn't income. Enter it in the GST field to add it to your total set-aside.

What are the 2026–27 tax rates?

For residents: nil up to $18,200, 15% to $45,000, 30% to $135,000, 37% to $190,000 and 45% above that, plus the 2% Medicare levy (ATO).

What is the small business income tax offset?

Eligible sole traders with aggregated turnover under $5 million can reduce tax by 16% of the tax on their net small business income, up to $1,000 a year (ATO).

When will I have to pay quarterly?

Once you've had business income, the ATO may put you into PAYG instalments, which are paid quarterly or annually. Setting aside each payday covers them.
