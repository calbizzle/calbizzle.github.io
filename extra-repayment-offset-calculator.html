How it's calculated

  1. Scheduled repayment = standard principal and interest formula on the balance, rate and term, with monthly compounding.
  2. Each month, interest = (balance − offset, minimum $0) × rate ÷ 12.
  3. Balance = balance + interest − (scheduled repayment + extra), repeated until the loan is paid off.
  4. Savings are compared with the same loan with no extras and no offset.

Worked example

$500,000 at 6% over 30 years costs $2,997.75 a month and about $579,191 in total interest. With $500,000 sitting in the offset, no interest is charged, and the repayments clear the loan in 167 months.

FAQ

How much does an offset account save?

Each dollar in a 100% offset reduces the balance that interest is charged on, so it saves interest at your loan rate, tax-free. At 6%, $50,000 in offset saves about $3,000 in the first year.

Is it better to make extra repayments or use an offset?

On the numbers they save the same interest. An offset keeps the money accessible. Extra repayments can sometimes only be withdrawn by redraw, which lenders can restrict. Investors should get tax advice, because the choice can affect deductibility.

Do extra repayments reduce my repayment amount?

Usually not. Most lenders keep your scheduled repayment the same, and you finish early. This calculator assumes that.

Are there limits on extra repayments?

Variable loans usually allow unlimited extra repayments. Fixed loans often cap them (for example $10,000 a year) and charge break costs. Check your loan terms.

Does this use my actual lender's method?

It uses monthly compounding and repayments, a common simplification. Lenders calculate interest daily and charge it monthly, so real results differ slightly.
